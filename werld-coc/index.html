Workshop on Expanding Research in Language Development (WERLD)

Code of Conduct

The WERLD course gives participants—instructors, attendees, and others—opportunities to learn, share, and connect with others. All attendees are expected to adhere to this code of conduct as closely as possible, which will help foster a safe, supportive, and respectful learning environment for everyone. Please read the code in its entirety before engaging with the course, and send any questions to werld2025@uchicago.edu

Guidelines for active participation in Zoom sessions

  • Whenever possible, log in 5 minutes before class time begins and plan to stay until the scheduled session is over. We will not delay the start of class.
  • Please make sure your Zoom avatar shows your full name, so that we can track attendance and keep track of contributions to discussion.
  • Before each class, please ensure that your laptop/phone has the latest Zoom updates, and that you have functioning camera, microphone, and speaker/headphone equipment.
  • Please plan to participate from a reasonably quiet area so that we can hear you when you talk.
  • To facilitate a more lively discussion, please keep your camera on as much as possible, in a position where we can clearly see your face when you talk and listen. This means you should plan ahead regarding where you sit during class time, what you are wearing, etc. Please plan to do whatever you need to feel comfortable keeping your camera on during class.
  • Please make sure your microphone is on mute if you’re not actively talking. If you forget, the Zoom host will probably mute you—don’t be alarmed!
  • Do not plan to multitask—plan to focus on class. Please don’t come to class while listening to or watching something else; please don’t take a phone call while we are in discussion; please don’t take us to the bathroom with you. You get the picture! If you need to take a break from focusing your full attention on class, please notify the instructor via private Zoom chat and then turn off your microphone and camera while you attend to personal business :)
  • Please clear your schedule during the duration of our class times. Treat this course as you would any in-person course and cancel conflicting appointments or plan to follow the course asychronously.

General expectations for conduct

We expect course participants to show meaningful engagement by reliably preparing for, attending, and contributing to discussion. We expect course participants to engage with each other with clear respect and professionalism. Finally, we expect that participants will put the learning goals of the group above their personal, individualized interests, in alignment with the participant-broadening aims of this course.

We will not tolerate any form of harassment. Examples of harassment can include: acts to intimidate others, demean others, invade others’ privacy, or make unwelcome advances to build relationships with others (sexual or otherwise). Please report harassment and other inappropriate behavior to werld2025@uchicago.edu. We will keep your report confidential.

About us

The chatter lab is founded on the principle that diversity in everyday language use is key to the study of human language cognition. We welcome opportunities to work with scholars from all walks of life, and particularly encourage those coming from marginalized groups to reach out if interested in working with the lab.

Our lab at the University of Chicago is located on the traditional homelands of the Ojibwe, Odawa, and Potawatomi nations.
